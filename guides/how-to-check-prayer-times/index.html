How to Check Prayer Times for Your City

Published August 3, 2026 · 4 min read

A prayer timetable is useful only when it belongs to the place where you are. That sounds obvious, but it is the point most often missed when someone saves a schedule, receives it in a message, or looks at a nearby city instead of their own.

Checking a timetable does not require turning every small difference into a problem. It means knowing what to compare, where a difference comes from, and when a local reference should take priority.

Begin with the actual city

Use the city where you will pray—not the closest well-known city and not a city that happens to be in the same governorate or region. Each city is tied to a geographical location and a time zone. Those two details determine the local time and the calculation for that day.

On CityTimeHub, a city page brings the day’s local time, date, next prayer, and full schedule into one view. It is a better starting point than a shared national table when you need a time for a specific place.

Compare the same day and the same method

When two schedules differ, first make sure that both are for the same date and the same city. Then look at the calculation method. Fajr and Isha are the prayers in which different recognised calculation methods most often produce a visible difference. Asr may also vary according to the school of jurisprudence followed.

A small difference does not automatically mean that one timetable is wrong. The important question is whether the source explains the method it uses. CityTimeHub explains its data source and calculation approach on the About page.

Know when to use a local reference

For everyday planning, a clearly sourced timetable is usually enough. For a mosque congregation, a local announcement remains the reference for its own schedule. This is not because the prayer time itself has become uncertain; it is because a congregation may set its own practical arrangements around the prayer time.

Use the right website tool for the question

If you are following several places, save them in the Cities Board instead of switching between unrelated pages. If travel or family abroad is the reason for checking, use Time Difference to compare two cities, and Monthly Prayer Times when you need to see a wider part of the month.

If you would like prayer reminders and after-prayer adhkar during the day, the CityTimeHub app extends what you begin on the website.

A calm way to check a timetable

  1. Open the page for the city you actually need.
  2. Confirm the date shown on that page.
  3. Read the calculation method if Fajr, Isha, or Asr differs from another source.
  4. For a mosque congregation, follow that mosque’s announced timetable.

This keeps the process practical: you use a reliable source for your city, understand the reason for a difference when one appears, and avoid treating every few minutes as a mystery.
